2. Controlling problem statement

The SOTA family has three linked defects:

  1. It does not enforce a sufficiently precise common contract for evidence, provenance, challenge, verification, final-state reporting, and human authority.
  2. Planning can introduce material remediation scope without first updating the issue or accepted report that authorized planning.
  3. Later plan and SOTA report versions can become change summaries instead of complete current artifacts.

The remedy must be the smallest contract that makes runs inspectable and final claims hard to misreport. A file, field, state, or tool becomes mandatory only when it maps to an issue criterion, a demonstrated failure, and a verifier test.

7. sota-slop-remove

Create an installed role skill adapted from the MIT-licensed Pstack unslop. Record the source URL, source commit, license, and adaptations.

Apply it to SOTA plans, preliminary reports, synthesis reports, verification narratives, and final responses. It must remove puffery, vague attribution, generic conclusions, chatbot phrases, filler, excessive hedging, forced triads, false ranges, abstract jargon, decorative formatting, and sentences that could be pasted unchanged into another report.

It must preserve every claim, evidence ID, citation, quotation, number, uncertainty, limitation, dissent, recommendation, verification result, and authority state. It must not rewrite code, data, schemas, paths, or source quotations. Compare the cleaned artifact against its frozen source findings; any semantic change fails.

Return the cleaned artifact and a short edit count. Do not generate a second prose report about the cleanup.
