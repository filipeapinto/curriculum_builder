# Independent LLM judge — a reviewer that is not the author

## Why this thread

The pipeline's only subjective quality gate never ran. `acceptance.json` for
all four units records `"routing_divergence": "USER_AUTHORIZED_IN_SESSION_MODEL;
cross-family judge bypassed"`, and `routing/authoring.json` gives the rationale:
`"user authorized the current in-session LLM as the model worker; no separate
API"`. The QA report states the consequence plainly:
`REV-JUDGE-SINGLE-CROSS-FAMILY` — "the only check that reads output like a
human would — never ran", and "it means the pipeline's sole subjective gate was
inert for the entire run."

So a single model authored four lessons and nothing read them back. The four
checks that did run (`LAB-SCHEMA-VALID`, `DOMAIN-SCHEMA-VALID`,
`DOMAIN-VERIFIER`, `RECEIPT-HASH-RESOLVES`) are integrity checks. None of them
can form an opinion.

The question this thread chases: if the judge had run, would running it inside
the same model family have been enough?

## Findings

**Judges systematically favour their own outputs, and also outputs from other
models in their own family.**
"Play Favorites: A Statistical Method to Measure Self-Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge"
(arXiv 2508.06709) builds a framework that "explicitly formalizes assumptions
under which self-bias can be identified and estimated", controlling for true
quality using independent human evaluation. It finds that "GPT-4o and Claude
3.5 Sonnet, systematically assign higher scores to their own outputs" and that
these models "display family-bias; systematically assigning higher ratings to
outputs produced by other models of the same family." Implication for this
pipeline: the `SINGLE-CROSS-FAMILY` qualifier in the check's own name is
earned. A judge drawn from the authoring family would have been measurably
generous, and the bypass note "no separate API" describes exactly the condition
under which teams reach for a same-family judge instead.

**The mechanism is familiarity, not correctness — which is why a
same-family judge fails silently rather than loudly.**
"Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge" (Wataoka, Takahashi, Ri; arXiv
2410.21819) introduces a quantitative metric for the bias and locates its cause
in perplexity: "LLMs assign significantly higher evaluations to outputs with
lower perplexity than human evaluators", and "the self-preference bias exists
because LLMs prefer texts more familiar to them." Note that this paper does
*not* propose cross-family judging as a remedy; it characterises the problem.
Implication for this pipeline: a biased judge does not return an obviously
broken verdict, it returns a plausible PASS. A green board from a same-family
judge is therefore not weak evidence of quality — it is no evidence.

**Multi-agent instructional-design systems in the literature still terminate in
review by someone other than the generator.**
"Instructional Agents" (arXiv 2508.19611, accepted ECAL'26, evaluated across
five university-level courses) "simulates role-based collaboration to ensure
pedagogical coherence" and offers Autonomous, Catalog-Guided, Feedback-Guided
and Full Co-Pilot modes, but describes its output as "reviewed and refined by
teaching faculty prior to use." Implication for this pipeline: even the
most autonomous published configuration keeps an independent reviewer at the
end. Our run kept none — the `*Draft pending downstream human review.*` line
hard-coded at `runtime/session_bridge.py:203` names a review stage that does
not exist in the pipeline.

**Perceived quality of AI-authored learning material can equal peer-authored
material while being conspicuously less varied.**
Denny, Khosravi, Hellas, Leinonen and Sarsa, "Can We Trust AI-Generated
Educational Content?" (arXiv 2306.10509), ran a blind student evaluation in
introductory programming and found "the quality of AI-generated resources, as
perceived by students, is equivalent to the quality of resources generated by
their peers", but that AI materials "tend to closely mirror the given
exemplars, whereas student-generated resources exhibit greater variety in terms
of content length and specific syntax features used." The equivalence is
student-perceived, not expert-adjudicated. Implication for this pipeline:
exemplar-mirroring is visible in our fixture as near-identical section
skeletons across L01-L04, and a judge that scores each unit in isolation will
never see it. The judge needs the set, not the unit.

## Discarded

- No source was discarded in this thread on quality grounds, but one was
  materially corrected before use. The search snippet for arXiv 2410.21819
  asserted that the paper recommends cross-family judging as the mitigation for
  self-preference bias. Fetching the abstract showed it proposes no mitigation
  at all — it measures the bias and attributes it to perplexity/familiarity.
  Had the snippet been trusted, this thread would have credited the wrong paper
  with the recommendation that justifies `REV-JUDGE-SINGLE-CROSS-FAMILY`. The
  claim now rests on arXiv 2508.06709, which does report family-bias
  empirically.
