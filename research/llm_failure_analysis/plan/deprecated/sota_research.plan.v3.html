SOTA Research Plan: Documenting LLM-Execution Failure

Research current mature practices for recording, reconstructing, explaining, and learning from failed or wasteful LLM execution, then determine what this repository should adopt.

Research date: August 2026Plan: version 3Status: awaiting approval

1. Research question

Primary research questionWhich mature mechanisms should this repository adopt or adapt to make failed, wasteful, or falsely successful LLM executions observable, explainable, reproducible, and useful for governing the next execution?

The reported incident—approximately 20% of weekly usage consumed overnight while producing graph-system documentation and revising a generic documentation skill—is the motivating case. The percentage, sequence, causes, and outcomes remain claims until supported by repository or usage evidence.

2. Research method

The work combines a systematic literature review of external evidence with a forensic reconstruction of the repository incident. PRISMA 2020 governs reporting of identification, screening, exclusion, and inclusion. Software-engineering SLR guidance governs the review protocol, search strategy, quality assessment, extraction, and synthesis.

EXTERNAL EVIDENCE

Systematic literature review

Identify and appraise standards, primary research, official technical documentation, mature open-source implementations, and documented industry practice.

INTERNAL EVIDENCE

Incident reconstruction

Reconstruct what ran, why it ran, what it consumed, what it produced, what controls operated, and which evidence is missing.

COMPARATIVE SYNTHESIS

Repository applicability

Compare external mechanisms against the demonstrated failure modes, current repository controls, implementation cost, and residual risk.

4. Research execution graph

The graph uses a limited BPMN 2.0 vocabulary. Circles are events, rounded rectangles are activities, diamonds are decisions or joins, solid arrows are sequence flows, and lanes identify responsibility. Human review is identified by task type and label; color carries no process meaning.

5. Evidence selection and appraisal

Candidate evidence classes

  1. Standards and primary specifications
  2. Peer-reviewed and original research
  3. Official technical documentation
  4. Mature open-source implementations and their tests
  5. Documented industry incidents and engineering practice

Protocol requirements

  • Exact sources, queries, dates, and retrieval records
  • Explicit inclusion and exclusion criteria
  • Duplicate removal with receipts
  • Quality criteria appropriate to each evidence class
  • Claim-to-source mapping
  • Contradictions and missing evidence retained
  • PRISMA selection counts

The preliminary study will determine whether the full review should treat observability records, causal analysis, cost controls, evaluation, durable execution, and governance as one synthesis or as focused subreviews. It will not assume that logs and traces are separate research domains.

No unavailable model may be silently substituted. Every model call records its exact model, effort, parent activity, input artifact identifiers, output artifact, elapsed time, retry status, and usage measures exposed by the platform.

8. Budget and stopping rules

The preliminary study receives a small human-approved ceiling. Its measured yield and cost are then used to estimate the full review.

Estimate from evidence

Use candidate yield, duplicate rate, screening time, extraction size, repository volume, model usage, and unresolved concept classes.

Authorize hard limits

Record maximum model calls, tokens, elapsed time, sources, concurrent workers, retries, and spend where available.

Stop or escalate

Stop at protocol completion, evidence saturation, repeated failure, limit threshold, inadequate evidence, or a decision requiring human authority.

A retry is permitted only after recording the failed activity and changing an input, hypothesis, executor, or method. Completed work is reused by stable identifier and digest.

11. Approval requested

Approval authorizes only the preliminary study described in Section 3, within a separately recorded numeric ceiling. The full systematic review begins only after the preliminary report, full protocol, team, model allocation, and budget are reviewed and approved.
