Formal Same-Model QA Gates for Codex

A SOTA research plan for finding defensible ways to gate high-intensity Codex work without relying on a different LLM, while being explicit about what process isolation can—and cannot—guarantee.

1. Question and intended decision

Primary research questionWhich formal, evidence-supported QA-gate architecture should this repository adopt for high-intensity Codex execution when both production and model-based review use Codex and no different LLM is available?

The intended decision is whether to implement a Codex-native gate, and if so which combination of deterministic checks, executable specifications, isolated review contexts, evidence-bound adjudication, audit records, stopping rules, and human authority is justified. The research must not assume that a new session, a new prompt, or a second sample is independent merely because it is separate.

DECISION USERS

Human owner and implementer

The human owner chooses the assurance tier and accepts residual risk. A later implementation team may use the approved reference architecture; this plan does not authorize implementation.

NON-CLAIM

No “unfakeable” promise

Same-model separation may reduce correlated error without eliminating it. The final report must distinguish reproducibility, procedural independence, model-family diversity, organizational independence, and cryptographic trust.

4. Research method

WORKSTREAM A

Systematic evidence review

Search, deduplicate, screen, appraise, and extract evidence on LLM-as-judge reliability, correlated errors, self-critique, verifier models, agent evaluation, executable specifications, software assurance, and auditability.

WORKSTREAM B

Local gate analysis

Reconstruct the existing gate’s claimed and actual controls, failure states, tests, trust assumptions, and transport dependencies. Separate verified behavior, attributed claims, inferences, and unknowns.

WORKSTREAM C

Controlled evaluation

Compare candidate architectures on a versioned fault corpus. Measure false-pass, false-block, abstention, evidence quality, reproducibility, latency, token/tool cost, and mutation score where applicable.

Search and eligibility protocol

  • Databases and sources proposed for calibration: ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, arXiv (original papers only), NIST and other standards bodies, official OpenAI documentation, official tool documentation, and primary repositories. Snowball backward/forward from included primary studies.
  • Concept blocks: (“LLM as judge” OR verifier OR self-critique OR self-refinement OR debate) AND (reliability OR bias OR calibration OR correlated error); plus (property-based OR metamorphic OR mutation testing OR proof obligation OR software assurance) AND (agent OR generated code OR document validation); plus provenance/audit-log and prompt-injection controls.
  • Include evidence that reports a mechanism, evaluable method, empirical result, formal property, or operational control relevant to gating. Exclude unsourced opinion, duplicate versions superseded by a fuller primary source, marketing claims without method, and work that cannot inform a QA decision.
  • Two-pass screening: reversible title/abstract triage followed by full-text eligibility. Every exclusion receives a reason. Borderline exclusions and all high-impact negative evidence require method-lead adjudication; the triage model has no final exclusion authority.

Appraisal, extraction, and synthesis

  • Appraise empirical studies for construct validity, benchmark representativeness, baselines, leakage/contamination controls, uncertainty, reproducibility, and applicability. Appraise standards/docs for authority, version, normative strength, scope, and testability. Appraise software for maturity, tests, maintenance, security posture, and demonstrated behavior.
  • Extract stable source IDs; mechanism; assurance claim; artifact type; model relationship; experimental design; sample; baseline; metrics and uncertainty; failure modes; resource cost; trust assumptions; applicability; and exact claim-to-source links. Preserve contradictions and null results.
  • Use structured comparative synthesis, not vote counting. If compatible quantitative results exist, report normalized effects with uncertainty; otherwise use direction-of-evidence and quality-stratified synthesis. No causal claim may exceed its study design.
  • Controlled evaluation must blind gate variants to seeded-fault labels, pin artifact/criteria versions, separate test generation from adjudication where possible, randomize order, repeat stochastic conditions, retain raw outputs, and use a deterministic scorer against gold labels. The protocol must predefine what counts as pass, fail, abstain, and invalid run.

6. Evidence questions and comparison framework

  1. What accuracy, bias, calibration, and correlated-failure evidence exists when an LLM judges outputs from itself or the same model family?
  2. Which separations—fresh context, hidden criteria, independent test generation, randomized sampling, panel aggregation, forced evidence, or adversarial roles—materially improve error detection, and under what conditions?
  3. Which deterministic or formal methods can bind a verdict to observable acceptance criteria for code, documents, plans, and mixed artifacts?
  4. How should a gate handle abstention, disagreement, missing evidence, prompt injection, non-convergence, mutable artifacts, and unavailable execution?
  5. Which assurance tiers are justified by artifact risk, and when is same-model QA insufficient without human or external authority?

11. Verification and acceptance criteria

  1. Both required HTML files exist at the exact locations, open standalone, have correct title/version/status, valid internal links, no missing cited local artifact, and render without clipped or overlapping content at desktop, mobile, and print widths.
  2. Every material factual claim and recommendation has a stable finding ID and direct support in the evidence map; attributed local claims, verified facts, inferences, alternatives, and unknowns remain distinct.
  3. Search, duplicate, screening, exclusion, inclusion, appraisal, and extraction counts reconcile. All exclusions have reasons; all included sources have appraisals and extractions.
  4. The evaluation is reproducible from the corpus manifest, frozen criteria, configurations, seeds, prompts, tool/model versions, raw outputs, and deterministic scoring records. Invalid runs and missing telemetry are visible.
  5. Candidate architectures are compared on false-pass, false-block, abstention, reproducibility, evidence quality, coverage or mutation score where applicable, latency, calls/tokens/cost, attack surface, and human escalation. Uncertainty and applicability limits are stated.
  6. No recommendation calls same-model review independent without qualification, claims cryptographic integrity from hashes alone, treats model consensus as truth, or lets a model waive a deterministic failure or approve its own material deviation.
  7. All independent-challenge findings have explicit dispositions. Any unresolved finding concerning false-pass risk, evidence sufficiency, security, feasibility, or misleading assurance is surfaced to the human owner and blocks acceptance.
  8. Budget ceilings, warnings, stops, retries, deviations, and actual executor/model/effort are reported and reconcile with ledgers. No silent overrun is acceptable.
  9. $sota-verify reports all required artifact, identifier, count, citation, link, schema, accessibility, rendering, integrity, budget, and challenge-disposition checks as passed. A failed check is repaired and reverified or explicitly blocks acceptance.
  10. The human owner reviews the final package and records accepted, rejected, or revision requested. Model completion, challenge, or verification alone is not acceptance.
