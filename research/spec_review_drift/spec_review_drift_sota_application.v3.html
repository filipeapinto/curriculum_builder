02 · What must be decided

The trigger contains three claims—not one

1

Loss of control

“I no longer have a complete grasp of the spec.” This is direct owner evidence that the current artifact exceeds the owner’s available decision model. It is not proof the content is wrong.

2

Economic exposure

Executing the specification may waste time and money. This means the relevant threshold is not scientific proof of drift; it is whether present confidence justifies an expensive action.

3

Intent divergence

The built result may differ from the initial intent after reciprocal AI review. This is the uncertain hypothesis the local evidence and SOTA must test—not assume.

Version shift
v1 → v2

What these facts establish: v11 specifies a rigorous repository product with real implementation surface—not merely a prose guide. What they do not establish: whether the owner deliberately chose that product architecture during earlier iterations.

05 · Full reasoning chain

From evidence to conclusion

  1. Full execution has meaningful cost and creates a three-skill repository system, schemas, fixtures, renderer integration, and operational gates. This follows directly from v11.
  2. The owner reports insufficient understanding to knowingly authorize that result. This follows directly from the trigger.
  3. The major product-shape change occurred early, and the available files show its content but not its authorization rationale. This follows from v1/v2 comparison and missing decision provenance.
  4. SOTA says reciprocal LLM review can improve or drift; it supplies no inherent fidelity guarantee. Therefore, neither “many reviews” nor “strong fixtures” resolves the authorization uncertainty.
  5. v11’s safeguards mainly govern artifacts produced after v11 is accepted. They do not independently validate acceptance of the governing three-skill product.
  6. Because error cost is material and a blind divergence test is cheaper and reversible, information gathering dominates full execution.
Conclusion strength: strong enough for “pause full execution”; not strong enough for “v11 is wrong,” “the agents drifted,” or “discard v11.”

07 · Discriminating action

A local probe tied to the actual ambiguity

01 · OWNER LANE

Describe outcomes without v11 vocabulary

Users, decisions supported, desired document, acceptable workflow, non-goals, dependencies, and spending limits.
02 · V1 LANE

Predict the product from early evidence

Use original prompts if recoverable plus v1; exclude later skill names, schemas, and renderer choices.
03 · V11 LANE

Predict concrete consequences

List files, skills, schemas, dependencies, approvals, actions, recurring costs, and what a successful run produces.
04 · DELTA DECISION

Show only material differences

The owner explicitly adopts, rejects, or defers each product-shape delta. Preserve dissent and evidence.
Decision rule: if the owner lane explicitly recognizes the three-skill workflow, repository-local renderer, artifact machinery, and cost profile, authorize one reversible vertical slice. If outcomes align but machinery does not, keep the documentation standard and redesign the execution architecture. If outcomes themselves diverge, reconstruct the governing intent before any build.
